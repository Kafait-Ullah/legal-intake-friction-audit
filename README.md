# Legal Intake Friction Audit

A practical, UPL-conscious checklist for finding leakage between a prospective client’s first inquiry and the firm’s next responsible action.

This is an operational audit, not legal advice. Each firm should adapt it to its jurisdiction, practice areas, ethics guidance, client-consent rules, data-retention policy, and approved technology vendors.

## The 15-point audit checklist

Score each item: **0 = absent**, **1 = partly defined**, **2 = documented and tested**.

### Response and access

1. **24/7 or defined coverage:** Is it clear who handles calls and website inquiries during business hours, meetings, lunch, evenings, weekends, and holidays?

1. **First-response target:** Does the firm have a measurable target for responding to phone, web, chat, and text inquiries?

1. **Fallback behavior:** If the primary person or system fails, does the call or form route to a real backup instead of disappearing into voicemail?

1. **Simple public intake path:** Can a prospective client understand how to contact the firm without legal or internal operations jargon?

### Qualification and routing

1. **Practice-area fit:** Are the minimum questions defined for each practice area the firm accepts?

1. **Conflict-check capture:** Does the workflow collect the names and entities needed for the firm’s approved conflict-screening process?

1. **Urgency rules:** Are custody, detention, imminent hearings, deadlines, safety issues, and other firm-defined urgent signals documented?

1. **Clear handoff:** Does every completed intake create a named next action, owner, due time, and escalation path?

1. **Consultation booking:** Can a qualified prospect book or request the next step without staff manually reconstructing the entire conversation?

### UPL, confidentiality, and data controls

1. **No-advice boundary:** Does the intake script avoid legal advice, legal conclusions, outcome promises, and strategy recommendations?

1. **Human escalation:** Does the workflow stop and route to a lawyer or authorized staff member when the caller asks for legal advice or presents an edge case?

1. **PII minimization:** Does the system collect only what is needed at intake and avoid unnecessary Social Security numbers, financial details, or sensitive narrative?

1. **Privacy and retention review:** Has the firm reviewed where recordings, transcripts, chats, and form data are stored, who can access them, and how long they are retained?

### Workflow and measurement

1. **Zero-dashboard routing:** Does intake data reach the approved CRM, case-management system, calendar, or alert channel without requiring staff to copy-paste the same information across multiple tabs?

1. **Weekly audit:** Does someone review missed calls, incomplete intakes, failed handoffs, wrong routing, response time, and booked consultations every week?

### Score interpretation

- **0–10:** The firm likely has material intake friction and should map the current workflow before buying another tool.

- **11–20:** The firm has pieces of a process, but failure points are probably person-dependent.

- **21–30:** The firm has a documented intake foundation. Test it with real edge cases and measure outcomes.

## The Law Firm Intake Leak Formula

Use this estimate in 60 seconds:

```
Monthly intake leakage
=
[(Missed inbound calls × qualification rate × retention rate)
+
(Slow or unanswered web leads × qualification rate × retention rate)]
×
Average collected matter value
```

### Define the inputs

- **Missed inbound calls:** Calls that reached voicemail, were abandoned, or were not handled within the firm’s target window.

- **Slow or unanswered web leads:** Forms, chats, or messages that did not receive a timely response.

- **Qualification rate:** The percentage of inquiries that would normally fit the firm’s practice and acceptance rules.

- **Retention rate:** The percentage of qualified inquiries that become paying matters under the firm’s normal process.

- **Average collected matter value:** Use collected revenue, not the headline fee or projected case value.

### Example

Assume a firm has:

- 80 missed calls and slow web leads in a month

- 25% qualification rate

- 40% retention rate

- $3,000 average collected matter value

```
[(80 × 0.25 × 0.40) × $3,000]
= $24,000 estimated monthly leakage
```

This is not a forecast or a benchmark. It is a sensitivity estimate. Replace every assumption with the firm’s actual call logs, CRM records, signed matters, and collected-fee data.

## How to run the audit

1. Export one month of call, web-form, chat, calendar, and CRM data.

1. Mark every inquiry as answered, missed, slow, qualified, booked, retained, referred, or unknown.

1. Score the 15 checklist items.

1. Calculate the leakage estimate using conservative assumptions.

1. Fix one front-door failure before buying another system.

1. Recheck the numbers after 30 days.

## Evidence and context

The framework is informed by a synthesis of 13 deep-dive conversations with practicing attorneys, legal operators, and a fractional CFO who works with more than 200 law firms. Public context includes Clio’s 2024 Legal Trends findings, Hennessey Digital’s 2025 lead-response study, and ABA coverage of the 2026 8am Legal Industry Report.
